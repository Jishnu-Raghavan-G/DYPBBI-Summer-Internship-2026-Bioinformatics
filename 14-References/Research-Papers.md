# Research Papers

This file lists the major research papers reviewed or referenced during the Bioinformatics Summer Internship 2026, with particular focus on the development of transcriptomic and molecular research in asthma.

## Asthma Transcriptomics Literature

The research-paper review component focused on how molecular and gene-expression research in asthma has developed over time.

Major themes included:

- Asthma-associated gene-expression signatures
- Differential gene expression
- Airway epithelial gene expression
- Disease-associated molecular pathways
- Molecular classification of asthma
- Hub genes and regulatory mechanisms
- Transcriptomic analysis
- Multicohort studies
- Clinical translation
- Precision-medicine approaches

---

## 1. Moffatt et al. — 2007

**Moffatt MF et al.**

Research investigating genetic factors associated with childhood asthma and the regulation of **ORMDL3** expression.

**Journal:** Nature  
**Year:** 2007  
**GEO Dataset:** GSE8052

This study represents an important early stage in the development of molecular approaches to understanding asthma.

---

## 2. Woodruff et al. — 2009

**Woodruff PG et al.**

Research examining gene-expression patterns in airway epithelial cells in relation to asthma.

**Journal:** American Journal of Respiratory and Critical Care Medicine  
**Year:** 2009  
**GEO Dataset:** GSE4302

The study contributed to understanding how airway epithelial gene-expression patterns can differ between asthma-related groups.

---

## 3. Peters et al. — 2014

**Peters MC et al.**

Research investigating molecular characteristics of asthma and airway inflammation using gene-expression approaches.

**Journal:** Journal of Allergy and Clinical Immunology  
**Year:** 2014

This work represents the increasing use of molecular profiling to investigate heterogeneity within asthma.

## 4. Nicodemus-Johnson et al. — 2016

Research examining molecular and gene-expression characteristics associated with asthma.

Research investigating genetic, transcriptomic, and epigenetic features relevant to asthma.

## 8. Lee et al. — 2025

**Lee I, Ganesan A, Kalesinskas L et al.**

*Multicohort Analysis of Bronchial Epithelial Cell Expression in Healthy Subjects and Patients with Asthma Reveals Four Clinically Distinct Clusters.*

**Journal:** American Journal of Respiratory Cell and Molecular Biology  
**Year:** 2025  
**Volume:** 73  
**Pages:** 73–87  
**GEO Dataset:** GSE115824

The study used multicohort transcriptomic analysis to identify clinically distinct molecular clusters in asthma and included an independent pediatric nasal-lavage validation cohort.
