# Biological Data

Bioinformatics deals with biological information generated from different experimental and computational methods. During the internship, I mainly worked with **gene expression data** and later connected it with protein and structural information.

## Major Types of Biological Data

### Genomic Data

Data related to DNA sequences, genes and genomes.

Examples include:

- DNA sequences
- Gene information
- Genome assemblies
- Genetic variants

### Transcriptomic Data

Data related to RNA and gene expression.

This was the main type of data used in the **asthma transcriptomics case study**. Publicly available expression data was obtained from the **NCBI Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO)**.

### Proteomic Data

Data related to proteins, including:

- Protein sequences
- Protein functions
- Protein interactions
- Protein structures

During the internship, protein information was explored using resources such as **UniProt, PDB and STRING**.

### Structural Data

Three-dimensional information about biological molecules, mainly proteins.

This became relevant during the structural bioinformatics and molecular docking modules, where I worked with:

- Experimental protein structures
- Predicted structures
- Homology models
- Protein–ligand structures

## From Data to Biological Meaning

A major point I learned was that biological data becomes useful when it is analysed and interpreted in the right context.

For example:

```text
Gene Expression Data
        ↓
Differentially Expressed Genes
        ↓
Functional Enrichment
        ↓
Pathway Analysis
        ↓
Protein Interaction Networks
        ↓
Protein Structure
        ↓
Molecular Interactions

The internship followed this general idea while studying asthma-related gene expression and then moving towards protein and molecular analysis.

Key Takeaway

Different types of biological data provide different levels of information.

DNA → RNA → Protein → Structure → Interaction

Understanding how these levels are connected is important for interpreting biological systems using computational methods.
