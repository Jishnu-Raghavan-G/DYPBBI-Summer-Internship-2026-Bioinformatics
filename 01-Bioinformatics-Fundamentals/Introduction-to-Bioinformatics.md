# Introduction to Bioinformatics

## What is Bioinformatics?

Bioinformatics is the use of computational methods to store, manage, analyse and interpret biological data.

It brings together biology, computer science, statistics and mathematics to work with large amounts of biological information.

During the internship, I understood bioinformatics mainly as a way of using computational tools to answer biological questions from data.

---

## Why Bioinformatics is Important

Modern biological research produces large amounts of data from techniques such as:

- DNA sequencing
- RNA sequencing
- Microarrays
- Proteomics
- Structural biology

Handling this amount of information manually is difficult, so computational methods are used to organise and analyse it.

Bioinformatics can help in areas such as:

- Disease research
- Gene expression analysis
- Biomarker identification
- Protein analysis
- Drug discovery
- Precision medicine

---

## Main Types of Biological Data

Some important types of data used in bioinformatics are:

### Genomic Data

Information about DNA sequences, genes and genomes.

### Transcriptomic Data

Information about RNA and gene expression.

During the internship, I worked mainly with **transcriptomic data** through the NCBI Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO).

### Proteomic Data

Information about proteins, their functions and interactions.

### Structural Data

Three-dimensional information about biological molecules such as proteins.

This became important later during the structural bioinformatics and molecular docking modules.

## Bioinformatics in the Internship

The internship showed how different computational resources can be connected into a single workflow.

The general progression was:

```text
Biological Data
      ↓
Database Exploration
      ↓
Gene Expression Analysis
      ↓
Functional Analysis
      ↓
Network Analysis
      ↓
Protein Analysis
      ↓
Structural Analysis
      ↓
Molecular Docking

For the asthma case study, publicly available gene expression data was obtained from GEO and analysed using GEO2R. The resulting genes were then studied using functional enrichment and protein–protein interaction analysis.

Later, protein information and structures were explored using resources such as UniProt, PDB, AlphaFold and SWISS-MODEL, followed by structural visualisation and molecular docking.

Key Takeaway

The main thing I learned was that bioinformatics is not just about using different software or databases.

The important part is connecting the computational result back to the biological question.

A typical analysis can move from:

Genes → Functions → Pathways → Networks → Proteins → Structures → Drug Interactions

This gave me a basic understanding of how computational biology can be used in modern biomedical research.
