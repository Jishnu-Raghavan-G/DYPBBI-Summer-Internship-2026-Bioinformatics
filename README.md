# DYPBBI Summer Internship 2026 — Bioinformatics

### Learning Portfolio & Research Documentation

> A structured record of my learning, practical work, analyses, research paper reviews, molecular docking learning, workshops, and final internship documentation from the Bioinformatics Summer Internship at Dr. D. Y. Patil Biotechnology & Bioinformatics Institute, Pune.

---

## About

This repository documents my **one-month Summer Internship in Bioinformatics** at **Dr. D. Y. Patil Biotechnology & Bioinformatics Institute (DYPBBI), Pune**, conducted from **1 July 2026 to 31 July 2026**.

The repository brings together the concepts I learned, biological databases and computational tools I explored, practical analyses, research paper reviews, molecular docking concepts, statistical methods, scientific research and writing skills, workshops, and the final internship report.

The internship provided exposure to a broad range of computational biology topics, progressing from **bioinformatics fundamentals and biological databases** to **transcriptomics, functional enrichment, network analysis, structural bioinformatics, molecular docking, virtual screening, statistics, and scientific research and communication**.

Instead of keeping the learning limited to the final internship report, I have organised the material into individual sections so that the concepts, workflows, analyses, and resources can be revisited and developed further through future projects and research.

Key Takeaways

The biggest thing I took away from the internship was understanding that bioinformatics is not simply about using individual databases or software tools.

A biological question can require multiple connected levels of analysis.

Genes
  ↓
Functions
  ↓
Pathways
  ↓
Protein Interactions
  ↓
Network Biology
  ↓
Protein Structures
  ↓
Protein–Ligand Interactions
  ↓
Potential Therapeutic Targets

I also learned that computational results need to be interpreted carefully.

Statistical thresholds, database evidence, network confidence, structural information, and docking predictions can all influence the interpretation of a result.

The software output is therefore only one part of the process. Understanding the biology behind the result is equally important.
