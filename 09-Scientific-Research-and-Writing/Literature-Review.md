# Literature Review

A literature review is the systematic process of finding, reading, organizing, comparing, and critically evaluating previously published scientific research related to a particular topic.

It helps establish what is already known, identify limitations in existing research, and provide context for a new investigation.

## 1. Purpose of a Literature Review

A literature review helps a researcher understand the current state of knowledge in a field.

Major purposes include:

- Understanding existing research
- Identifying important findings
- Learning commonly used methodologies
- Identifying research gaps
- Comparing results from different studies
- Understanding disagreements or limitations
- Developing a research question
- Providing scientific context for a study

A simplified workflow is:

```text
Research Topic
      ↓
Literature Search
      ↓
Relevant Papers
      ↓
Read and Organize
      ↓
Compare Findings
      ↓
Identify Gaps
      ↓
Research Question
2. Starting a Literature Review

The first step is to clearly define the topic or research question.

For example:

Broad Topic:
Asthma

        ↓

Specific Area:
Asthma-related gene expression

        ↓

Focused Question:
Which genes show altered expression in
asthma-associated samples?

A focused topic makes the literature search more efficient.

3. Searching Scientific Literature

Scientific literature can be searched using:

PubMed
Google Scholar
NCBI
Research databases
Institutional repositories
Journal websites

Useful search terms should represent the main concepts of the research question.

For example:

Asthma
+
Gene Expression
+
Transcriptomics

Search terms can then be combined or refined to obtain more relevant studies.

4. Keywords

Keywords are important because they determine which studies are retrieved during a literature search.

A good keyword strategy may include:

Disease / Condition
        +
Biological Process
        +
Method
        +
Molecular Target

Example:

Asthma
+
Transcriptomics
+
Gene Expression
+
Microarray

Synonyms and related terms can also be included to improve the search.

5. Selecting Relevant Papers

Not every paper found during a search will be relevant.

Papers can initially be screened using:

Title
Abstract
Keywords
Publication information
Study objectives
Methods

A basic selection workflow is:

Search Results
      ↓
Title Screening
      ↓
Abstract Screening
      ↓
Full-Text Evaluation
      ↓
Relevant Studies
6. Reading a Research Paper

A research paper can be approached systematically.

A useful reading order is:

Title
  ↓
Abstract
  ↓
Introduction
  ↓
Figures / Tables
  ↓
Results
  ↓
Discussion
  ↓
Methods
  ↓
References

The exact reading order can vary depending on the purpose of the review.

7. Understanding the Abstract

The abstract provides a short overview of the study.

It commonly includes:

Background
Research objective
Methods
Major findings
Conclusion

The abstract helps determine whether a paper is relevant enough to read in detail.

8. Understanding the Introduction

The introduction provides the scientific background of the study.

It usually explains:

What is already known
Why the topic is important
Existing limitations
The research problem
The objective of the study

The introduction helps establish the motivation for the research.

9. Understanding the Methods

The methods section explains how the study was conducted.

Depending on the type of research, it may describe:

Study population
Experimental procedures
Data sources
Biological samples
Computational tools
Statistical methods
Analysis parameters

For bioinformatics studies, methods may include:

Dataset
   ↓
Data Processing
   ↓
Statistical Analysis
   ↓
Functional Analysis
   ↓
Network Analysis
10. Understanding Results

The results section presents the findings obtained from the study.

Important elements may include:

Tables
Figures
Statistical results
Differentially expressed genes
Pathways
Protein interactions
Experimental measurements

When reading results, it is useful to distinguish between:

What was observed?
        ↓
What was statistically supported?
        ↓
What biological interpretation was proposed?
11. Understanding the Discussion

The discussion interprets the results in relation to existing scientific knowledge.

It may address:

Main findings
Possible mechanisms
Comparison with previous studies
Biological significance
Limitations
Future research

A literature review should pay attention to how authors connect their findings with previous research.

12. Critical Reading

A literature review should not simply summarize papers.

Researchers should critically evaluate the evidence.

Questions to consider include:

What was the research question?
Was the methodology appropriate?
Was the sample size adequate for the study?
Were appropriate controls used?
Were the statistical methods appropriate?
Are the conclusions supported by the results?
What limitations were reported?
Are there alternative explanations?

A useful framework is:

Claim
 ↓
Evidence
 ↓
Method
 ↓
Results
 ↓
Interpretation

14. Identifying Research Gaps

One of the important goals of a literature review is identifying areas that require further investigation.

A research gap may arise from:

Limited sample sizes
Incomplete datasets
Conflicting findings
Unexplored biological mechanisms
Lack of experimental validation
Limitations in existing methodologies
Understudied populations or conditions

A simplified process is:

Existing Studies
       ↓
Compare Findings
       ↓
Identify Limitations
       ↓

       ↓
Research Gap

18. Research Notes

Taking structured notes makes it easier to compare papers later.

For each paper, record:

Research Question
        ↓
Dataset / Samples
        ↓
Methods
        ↓
Major Results
        ↓
Biological Interpretation
        ↓
Limitations
        ↓

This prevents repeatedly rereading papers to locate important information.

19. Avoiding Plagiarism

Information obtained from scientific papers should not be copied directly into a research document without appropriate attribution.

A good workflow is:

Read Source
    ↓
Understand the Idea
    ↓
Take Notes
    ↓
Write in Your Own Words
    ↓
Cite the Original Source

Direct quotations, where necessary, should be clearly identified and appropriately cited.

20. Citation During Literature Review

Scientific claims based on previous research should be supported by appropriate citations.

For example:

Scientific Claim
       ↓
Original Research Paper
       ↓
Citation
       ↓
Reference List

Using the original research paper is generally preferable when discussing a specific experimental finding.

21. Literature Review vs Annotated Bibliography

These are related but different.

Literature Review

Synthesizes and critically evaluates multiple sources.

Paper 1
   +
Paper 2
   +
Paper 3
   ↓
Comparison
   ↓
Synthesis
   ↓
Research Context
Annotated Bibliography

Provides individual entries describing and evaluating each source separately.

Paper 1 → Annotation
Paper 2 → Annotation
Paper 3 → Annotation

A literature review goes beyond individual summaries by connecting findings across studies.

22. Literature Synthesis

Synthesis means combining information from multiple studies to develop a broader understanding.

For example:

Paper 1
→ Gene A associated with inflammation

Paper 2
→ Gene A associated with immune response

Paper 3
→ Gene A associated with disease severity

             ↓

Synthesis:
Gene A may have relevance to multiple
biological aspects of the disease.

The synthesis should distinguish established evidence from hypotheses that require further investigation.

23. Common Problems in Literature Reviews
Excessive Description

Simply summarizing one paper after another does not create a strong synthesis.

Poor Source Selection

Irrelevant or unreliable sources can weaken the review.

Ignoring Contradictory Findings

Different studies may produce different results. These differences should be examined rather than ignored.

Unsupported Claims

Scientific statements should be supported by appropriate evidence.

Poor Organization

A literature review should have a logical structure.

Citation Errors

Sources should be cited accurately and consistently.

26. Key Takeaways
A literature review examines and synthesizes existing scientific research.
A focused research question makes literature searching more effective.
Scientific databases and search engines can be used to locate relevant papers.
Papers should be evaluated for relevance before detailed reading.
Critical reading involves examining methods, evidence, results, and limitations.
Multiple papers should be compared rather than treated as isolated summaries.
Literature synthesis helps identify broader patterns and relationships.
Research gaps can emerge from limitations, contradictions, or unanswered questions.
Proper citation is essential when using information from previous research.
Structured notes improve organization and reduce repeated work.
A strong literature review provides scientific context for future research.
