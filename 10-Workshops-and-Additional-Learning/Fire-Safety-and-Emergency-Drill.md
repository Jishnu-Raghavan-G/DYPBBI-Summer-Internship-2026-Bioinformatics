# Fire Safety and Emergency Drill

## Introduction

Fire safety is an important part of laboratory safety because laboratories may contain electrical equipment, chemicals, heating devices, compressed gases, and other potential sources of fire.

Emergency drills provide practical awareness of how to respond during an emergency. They help individuals become familiar with evacuation procedures, emergency exits, fire-safety equipment, and appropriate responses to hazardous situations.

---

## Objectives

The fire-safety and emergency-drill activity was intended to develop awareness of:

- Fire hazards
- Fire prevention
- Emergency response
- Evacuation procedures
- Emergency exits
- Fire-fighting equipment
- Personal safety
- Communication during emergencies

---

## Common Laboratory Fire Hazards

Potential sources of fire in laboratory environments can include:

- Flammable chemicals
- Electrical equipment
- Heating equipment
- Open flames
- Gas systems
- Faulty electrical connections
- Improper chemical storage

The actual hazards depend on the laboratory and the activities being performed.

---

## Fire Prevention

Fire prevention begins with identifying and controlling potential sources of ignition and combustible materials.

Important practices include:

- Store chemicals appropriately.
- Keep flammable materials away from ignition sources.
- Use electrical equipment correctly.
- Avoid damaged electrical cables.
- Do not leave heating equipment unattended when prohibited by laboratory procedures.
- Maintain clear emergency pathways.
- Follow laboratory safety protocols.

---

## Emergency Exits

Emergency exits provide a safe route out of a building during an emergency.

Laboratory personnel should know:

- Where the nearest emergency exit is located
- Which routes can be used for evacuation
- Where emergency assembly areas are located
- Which routes should remain unobstructed

Emergency exits should never be blocked by equipment, boxes, or other materials.

---

## Emergency Evacuation

During a fire or other emergency requiring evacuation, the primary objective is to move people safely away from danger.

A simplified evacuation sequence is:

```text
Recognize Emergency
        ↓
Raise / Receive Alarm
        ↓
Stop Work if Safe to Do So
        ↓
Leave Through Appropriate Exit
        ↓
Move to Assembly Area
        ↓
Follow Emergency Instructions

The exact procedure should follow the institution's emergency plan.

Fire Alarm

A fire alarm provides an immediate warning of a potential fire or other emergency.

When an alarm is activated, individuals should follow the established emergency procedure rather than assuming that the situation is minor.

Important actions may include:

Stop work when safe.
Leave the area.
Follow evacuation instructions.
Use designated exits.
Assist others when appropriate and safe.
Proceed to the designated assembly point.
Fire Extinguishers

Fire extinguishers are designed to control certain types of small fires.

Different extinguishers are suitable for different classes of fire.

The correct extinguisher depends on the material that is burning.

Examples of fire categories can include:

Ordinary combustible materials
Flammable liquids
Flammable gases
Electrical equipment
Certain combustible metals

The classification system may vary by country and fire-safety standard.

Using a Fire Extinguisher

A person should only attempt to use a fire extinguisher when:

The fire is small and manageable.
The person has been appropriately trained.
A safe escape route remains available.
The correct extinguisher is available.
Using the extinguisher does not create additional danger.

If these conditions are not met, evacuation should take priority.

A commonly taught extinguisher procedure is:

PASS

P → Pull the pin
A → Aim at the base of the fire
S → Squeeze the handle
S → Sweep from side to side

This procedure should only be used where appropriate and after suitable training.

Electrical Fires

Electrical equipment can present a fire hazard.

If electrical equipment is involved in a fire:

Do not use water unless specifically confirmed safe.
Raise the alarm.
Evacuate if necessary.
Follow the institution's emergency procedure.
Only trained personnel should attempt fire suppression.

Electrical equipment should be disconnected only when this can be done safely.

Chemical Fires

Chemical fires require particular caution because different chemicals can behave differently during combustion.

The response should depend on:

Chemical identity
Fire classification
Safety Data Sheet information
Available fire-suppression equipment
Institutional emergency procedures

Untrained individuals should prioritize evacuation and notification rather than attempting to manage an unknown chemical fire.

Emergency Drill

An emergency drill is a controlled exercise designed to practice the response to a simulated emergency.

A drill may involve:

Alarm
  ↓
Recognition of Emergency
  ↓
Evacuation
  ↓
Movement to Assembly Point
  ↓
Head Count / Accountability
  ↓
Instructions and Debrief

The exact procedure depends on the institution's emergency plan.

Importance of Emergency Drills

Emergency drills help participants:

Become familiar with evacuation routes
Understand alarm procedures
Practice orderly evacuation
Identify emergency equipment
Reduce confusion during real emergencies
Understand their responsibilities
Improve emergency preparedness

A drill is a learning exercise and should be treated seriously.

Assembly Point

After evacuation, individuals should proceed to the designated assembly point.

The assembly area allows responsible personnel to:

Account for people
Communicate instructions
Identify missing individuals
Prevent people from unnecessarily returning to the building

Individuals should not leave the assembly area until authorized.

Do Not Re-Enter

After evacuation, individuals should not return to the affected building unless authorized by the appropriate emergency personnel.

Even when a fire appears to be under control, hazards may remain.

Emergency Communication

Clear communication is important during an emergency.

Relevant information may include:

Location of the incident
Type of hazard
Whether anyone is injured
Whether evacuation has occurred
Whether additional assistance is required

Emergency information should be communicated through the appropriate institutional channels.

Personal Safety

During an emergency:

Stay calm.
Follow instructions.
Avoid unnecessary risks.
Do not attempt to collect personal belongings.
Use designated exits.
Do not use unsafe routes.
Assist others only when it is safe to do so.
Report to the designated assembly area.

Personal safety should take priority over equipment, samples, or other laboratory materials.

Laboratory Emergency Equipment

Depending on the facility, emergency equipment may include:

Fire extinguishers
Fire alarms
Emergency exits
First-aid kits
Emergency showers
Eyewash stations
Spill-response equipment
Emergency communication systems

The location and correct use of this equipment should be understood before an emergency occurs.

A simple principle for laboratory emergencies is:

Recognize

Protect

The specific actions depend on the type and severity of the emergency.
